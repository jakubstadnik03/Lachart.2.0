The science behind your test —
and proof you're improving.

LaChart finds your thresholds with several methods at once, compares your tests over time, and tracks how your fitness is trending between them. Here's what's under the hood.

🔬 The science

How LaChart reads your test.

No single threshold method is right for every athlete, so LaChart doesn't pick one — it runs several and shows you where they agree. Fewer arguments about "which method," more confidence in the number.

8 methods
Threshold detection

LT1 and LT2 from several methods at once

D-max, OBLA, log-log, IAT and more run on your curve together, so you can see the consensus instead of trusting one algorithm's opinion. Adjust anything by hand and everything downstream updates.

  • Multiple methods — D-max, OBLA, log-log, IAT and others
  • See the consensus — where the methods agree on LT1/LT2
  • Fully editable — override a point, zones follow
Then vs. now
Test comparison

Put two tests side by side and watch yourself improve

Overlay any two tests to see the curve shift, the threshold move and the zones change. It's the clearest proof that a training block worked — or the early warning that it didn't.

  • Overlay curves from any two dates
  • Threshold & zone deltas spelled out
  • Season view — your progression at a glance
📈 Progress

Know your threshold is moving — between tests.

You can't test every week, so LaChart watches your training in between and tells you when your thresholds have likely shifted.

Trend tracking
Threshold trend & drift

A heads-up when it's time to re-test

LaChart tracks how your threshold is trending from the training you log and quietly flags when the drift is big enough to matter — so you re-test with purpose instead of guessing whether your zones are stale.

  • Trend line — your threshold over the whole season
  • Drift detection — a nudge when zones look outdated
  • Re-test with intent — confirm the change, reset zones
